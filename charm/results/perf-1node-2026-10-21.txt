What changed (c185cda), one PE, 400k bodies of the same input:
  traversal 2.81 -> 1.01 s/step, step 3.03 -> 1.24
  - before: 93% of the step was the walk, 8.9 ns per interaction; the
    kernel was scalar SSE2 with a sqrt and two divides per interaction and
    stored each target's sums to memory after every source
  - interaction-list kernel vectorised with AVX2 (-march=native,
    -fno-math-errno, -fno-trapping-math): 2.81 -> 1.18
  - devirtualised walk, fixed-array stack: 1.18 -> 1.01

Still open:
  - leaf 1 is 5.3x slower than Julia at 64 cores. Each leaf walks the
    tree alone: 770M node visits per step at 400k bodies, against 53M at
    leaf 10. Julia walks groups of particles together with a per-particle
    test. A group walk that keeps our per-bucket test gives the same
    interactions (results to roundoff) at a fraction of the visits.
  - at leaf 10 the walk (node visits) is now ~2x the kernel time on one PE.
  - cost-based balancing (-balance=cost) does not change the steady-state
    step at 16-120 PEs (within 3%).
  - step 0 is slower than later steps at high PE counts (1x120: 0.88 s vs
    0.35 s), so averages that include step 0 overstate the step time.
