NOTES

Traversal is the phase that scales. At 2M it goes 16.251 -> 0.239 s/step from
1 to 120 cores, a 68x speedup at 57% efficiency; under weak scaling it is
nearly flat, 0.345 -> 0.592 s/step over a 120-fold increase in both cores and
bodies. Tree build is what bends the total curve, and it is now a small
absolute cost (0.05-0.15 s/step at 2M) rather than the dominant one.

Multi-process beats single-process everywhere above 30 cores: on the full
node by 1.9x at 2M (0.447 against 0.846) and 1.7x at 8M (1.528 against 2.651). In the superseded sweep the opposite held,
by a factor of 3.6; that was the broken fabric path, not tree-build cost.

Best 2M time is 0.427 s/step at 105 cores (40x). 120 cores is marginally
slower, 0.447, as tree build grows faster than traversal shrinks.

CAVEAT ON ACCURACY. Potential energy shows intermittent one-step excursions on
the oct-tree path, up to 4e-3 relative, reproducible per configuration and
varying with PE count. It is pre-existing, not from the Node.h patch: patched
and unpatched HEAD are bit-identical wherever unpatched HEAD runs. The
particle-count decomposition of 15ad03f does not show it. See FIXES.md. The
timings above are unaffected, but the oct-tree path should not be treated as
numerically validated until this is understood.
